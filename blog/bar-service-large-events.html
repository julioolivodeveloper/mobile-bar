How to Plan Bar Service
for 200+ Guests in Michiana, Indiana

June 25, 2025  ·  Lemon & Lime Bar Mobile

Serving 200 guests is a fundamentally different challenge than serving 50. The logistics, the quantities, the staffing, the timing — everything scales in ways that catch first-time large event planners off guard. A bar line that moves fine for 80 people can turn into a 20-minute wait for 200, which kills the energy of any party fast. If you're planning a large event in Michiana — a big quinceañera, a wedding reception, a family reunion, a graduation party — here's what you need to think through before you book your bar service.

Start Planning Early — Very Early

For events of 200 or more guests, we recommend reaching out to your mobile bar provider at least 6 to 8 weeks in advance, ideally more. Large events require more planning time: quantity calculations, staffing coordination, logistics review, and menu customization all take longer at scale. The bar providers who can handle large events are often booked well in advance, especially during peak season (May through October). Don't wait.

How Many Drinks Will You Need?

This is the question everyone wants answered, and there's a reliable formula for it. Use this as your starting point:

Total drinks needed = Guests × Hours of service × 1.5 drinks per hour

So for 200 guests at a 4-hour event: 200 × 4 × 1.5 = 1,200 drinks. That number sounds large, but it accounts for guests who drink more, guests who drink less, and peak demand periods where everyone wants a drink at once. Underpreparing for a large event is one of the most common and embarrassing mistakes — running out of drinks an hour before the party ends is something guests remember.

Keep in mind that not everyone drinks alcohol. For a typical large family event, roughly 20-30% of guests may prefer non-alcoholic options. This affects your cocktail-to-mocktail/lemonade ratio and should be factored into your planning.

The Importance of a Wide Menu

Large events bring diverse crowds. At a wedding or quinceañera with 200 guests, you might have people ranging from teenagers to grandparents, from craft cocktail lovers to guests who only drink beer. A narrow menu that only offers two or three options will leave large segments of your crowd underserved.

We recommend a menu that covers several bases: a signature cocktail, a beer-based option (Michelada), a spirit-forward option (Margarita, Paloma, or Vampiro), a non-alcoholic mocktail, and a kids/non-drinker option like fresh lemonade. This way, no matter who walks up to the bar, there's something perfect for them.

Staffing: One Bartender Per 75–100 Guests

This is the industry standard and it exists for good reason. A skilled bartender can make roughly 40 to 60 cocktails per hour — but they also have to manage ice, restock, handle card payments (if applicable), and interact with guests. At 200 guests, you need a minimum of two bartenders, ideally three for peak demand periods.

When you speak with a mobile bar service, ask directly: How many bartenders will you bring for 200 guests? If the answer is one, that's a red flag. Long lines kill party energy, and your guests shouldn't have to wait 15 minutes for a drink.

Setup Logistics and Space Requirements

A professional mobile bar setup requires adequate space and access. Plan for:

  • A flat, stable surface of approximately 8 to 12 feet of bar frontage
  • Access to a water source nearby (for rinse buckets and cleanup)
  • Electrical access for blenders, lighting, and any refrigeration equipment
  • Vehicle access to the setup area for loading in supplies and equipment
  • Enough space for a bartender workspace behind the bar that guests don't accidentally walk through

Share your venue layout with your bar provider during the planning phase. We've set up at everything from large banquet halls to open fields in Michiana, and we're good at problem-solving space challenges — but we need to know what we're working with ahead of time.

Timing: When Should the Bar Open and Close?

For large events, bar timing matters more than most hosts realize. Open the bar too early and guests drift away from the formal program; too late and they get restless. Our general recommendations:

  • Open the bar 15 to 20 minutes after guests arrive and the initial welcome has happened
  • Pause service briefly during key moments (first dance, toast, cutting the cake) so people are paying attention, not waiting in line
  • Close the bar 30 to 45 minutes before the event officially ends — this gives guests time to finish their last drinks, prevents late-night overconsumption, and gives our team time to begin breaking down

Mocktail and Non-Alcoholic Options for Large Mixed Crowds

At large events, the percentage of guests who don't drink alcohol is often higher than hosts expect. Pregnant guests, designated drivers, minors, and guests with health or religious reasons for not drinking all need something beyond a glass of water. Mocktails — drinks that mirror the flavor and presentation of cocktails without the alcohol — are a powerful solution. A Virgin Mojito with mint and lime soda, a sparkling lemonade with tajín, or a fruit agua fresca can be just as festive and satisfying as their alcoholic counterparts.

At Lemon & Lime Bar Mobile, our non-alcoholic options are given the same attention and quality as our cocktails. Every guest deserves to feel included at the bar.
